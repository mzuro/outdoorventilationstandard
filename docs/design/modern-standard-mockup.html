Start with your question

Plain-language answers first. Each one traces to the research brief and instrument that back it.

2.1

What CFM do I need?

Not a rule of thumb. Airflow only matters once the hood face can geometrically intercept the plume — width and height come first, CFM is derived after.

RB-008 · Instrument I-01 2.2

Does wind matter?

More than any other variable. A steady 5 mph breeze can displace the plume by half a hood-width before it reaches the capture plane. Try it in Figure 1.

RB-006 · Instrument I-03 2.3

Island or wall hood?

A wall behind the grill is a free side panel: it blocks one entrainment path and one wind direction. Islands need wider hoods, mounted lower, to match.

RB-005, RB-009 · Instrument I-04 2.4

How high should the hood be?

Every added inch costs capture twice — the plume gets wider and slower. Mounting height is the strongest geometric lever you control.

RB-002, RB-003 · Instrument I-02 2.5

Do side panels actually help?

In wind, yes — more than any realistic CFM increase. Panels convert an exposed plume into a partially channeled one and cut crossflow losses sharply.

RB-009 · Instrument I-06 2.6

Can I use an indoor hood outdoors?

The hardware might survive; its sizing rules will not. Indoor guidance assumes still air and enclosing walls. Outdoors, both assumptions fail.

RB-004 · Instrument I-07
