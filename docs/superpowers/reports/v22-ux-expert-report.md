## Executive summary

The v2/v2.1 build already does the hardest thing right: **the answer-first pattern is real** — measured time-to-answer for "48-inch island hood CFM" from a cold mobile landing is ~30–45 seconds, inside the 60-second budget, and the numbers are visible without touching a single control (which the interaction-rate literature says is exactly correct). The instruments are honest, keyboard-operable, reduced-motion-clean, and dark-mode first-class.

What's broken is *legibility of the interactive layer and the mobile chrome*: at 375px the nav hides 60% of the site behind an unsignaled horizontal scroll (P0-1); instrument readouts update off-screen when a phone user touches a control, severing the feedback loop that justifies the instruments' existence (P0-2); and the v2.1 fun-physics features — drags, preset chips, verdict stamps — are systematically under-signaled, so the delight that was built mostly won't be found (P1-1/2/3). Finally, the homeowner's *actual* question — "is the hood I'm considering enough?" — is answerable with zero new physics via a user-supplied-CFM comparison on I-02 (P1-4), turning the site's most-visited instrument into a decision-closer instead of a lookup.

## 1. Research findings (sourced)

### 1.1 Explorable explanations — what the lineage actually says

- **Bret Victor (the founding text):** a reactive document lets readers *"play with the author's assumptions and analyses, and see the consequences"* — text as *"an environment to think in."* But the load-bearing caveat is authorial guidance: *"the author must guide the reader, and provide a structure for the learning experience."* Interactive widgets without guidance are *"abandoned sandboxes."* Source: [Explorable Explanations, worrydream.com](https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/) (fetched 2026-07-14).
- **Nicky Case (current practice):** playtesting shows readers *skim*; counterintuitively *"by withholding some content, the explorer learned more"* (content gating / progressive complexity). Teach mechanics in isolation before combining; put the full sandbox at the *end*, with everything before it introducing pieces. Best structure: hook → guided pieces → sandbox payoff. Sources: [Explorable Explanations, blog.ncase.me](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations/); [4 More Design Patterns](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations-4-more-design-patterns/) (fetched 2026-07-14).
- **Distill, "Communicating with Interactive Articles" (Hohman, Conlen, Heer, Chau, 2020 — still the canonical survey):** interactivity has real costs — *"interactivity may be distracting to readers or the functionality may go unused."* Effective patterns: **details-on-demand to prevent cognitive overload**, **prompting readers to make a prediction** before revealing (self-explanation effect), and multiple linked representations (*"people learn better from words and pictures rather than words or pictures alone"*). Source: [distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/) (fetched 2026-07-14).

**Read for OVS:** the instruments are architecturally right (physics-honest, integrated with prose on question pages). The gap the lineage predicts is *guidance*: an instrument dropped on a page with 4+ controls and no "try this first" is Victor's abandoned sandbox. Preset chips are exactly the right medicine **if** they read as invitations (see §1.2) — and the "predict before reveal" pattern is an untapped, cheap engagement win that requires no new physics.

### 1.2 Do readers even touch interactive things? (the Tse/Aisch evidence)

- **NYT's Archie Tse (Malofiej 2016), "Why we are doing fewer interactives":** *"readers just want to scroll"*; steppers/tabs/sliders meant *"readers weren't getting to all of the content."* His rules: **"If you make a tooltip or rollover, assume no one will ever see it"** and **"if the content is important for readers to see, don't hide it."** Sources: [Nieman Lab coverage, Mar 2016](https://www.niemanlab.org/2016/03/at-the-malofiej-infographics-world-summit-the-best-form-of-storytelling-is-often-static/); [Dominikus Baur, "The death of interactive infographics?"](https://medium.com/@dominikus/the-end-of-interactive-visualizations-52c585dcafcb).
- **Gregor Aisch's rebuttal ("In defense of interactive graphics," 2017):** low interaction rates don't mean no interactivity — they mean **"you should not hide important content behind interactions."** Source: [via FlowingData, Apr 2017](https://flowingdata.com/2017/04/05/interactive-visualization-is-still-alive/); original at driven-by-data.net.
- **Distill's synthesis:** the NYT finding (*"only a fraction of readers interact with non-static content"*) became an over-generalized rule of thumb; other research found *"many readers, even those on mobile devices, are interested in utilizing interactivity when it is a core part of the article's message."* Source: [Distill 2020](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/).

**Read for OVS (this is the governing design law for the whole site):** every number a persona needs must be **visible in the default state** — the default render of each instrument, and the first paragraph of each question page, must already contain the answer. Interaction is for *verification and delight*, never for *retrieval*. Any verdict/readout that only appears after a drag is a defect. This also aligns perfectly with the physics-truthfulness constraint: the default state is a real computed state.

### 1.3 Affordance discoverability — do users realize they can drag?

- **Flat/minimal design weakens perceived affordances**: interactive elements become hard to distinguish from static content; ghost-style borders reduce perceived clickability; "false affordances" (looks interactive, isn't) actively damage trust. Signal interactivity with borders, color, cursor, placement, and **visible handles / motion hints** ("discoverability affordances: visible handles, bouncing animations, partially visible content"). Sources: [UXPin, Affordances in UX Design, 2026](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/affordances-user-interaction/); [Clemson study on affordance-based clickability cues](http://andrewd.ces.clemson.edu/courses/cpsc412/fall17/teams/reports/group04.pdf).
- **Touch gestures are the worst case**: *"unless users have prior knowledge that a gesture exists, they won't try"* — hidden gestures must be memorized; the accepted mitigations are subtle visual cues, one-time animated hints, and never making the gesture the only path. Sources: [Nick Babich, In-app Gestures and Mobile App Usability, UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/in-app-gestures-and-mobile-app-usability-d2e737bd5250); [Smashing Magazine, In-App Gestures and Mobile UX, 2016](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2016/10/in-app-gestures-and-mobile-app-user-experience/); [GhostUI (arXiv 2026) — "the prevalence of exclusively hidden functionality highlights a systematic discoverability gap"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19258).
- I could find **no quantitative 2025-2026 study specifically measuring "% of users who discover an in-SVG drag affordance"** — the honest position is: assume near-zero unaided discovery on touch, low on desktop, and design hints accordingly. **[no primary quantitative source]**

**Read for OVS:** the v2.1 drag features (hood-edge, wind-arrow) are delight for persona (b), but research says they are invisible by default. They need: cursor changes (desktop), a one-time subtle motion hint (e.g., handle pulse on first viewport entry, suppressed under reduced-motion), and visible handle geometry — not just widened invisible hit strips. Because sliders remain the primary path (binding constraint), drags are pure upside *if hinted* and harmless if never found — Aisch's condition is met as long as no state is drag-only.

### 1.4 Homeowner decision-support — the anxious buyer, not the physics student

- **Calculator/decision-tool UX**: clarity over complexity; immediate feedback; **avoid computing before the user has given input** (feels presumptuous / erodes trust) — but equally, defaults should make the first meaningful answer 1–2 touches away; explain *why* each input is needed; progressive disclosure of advanced inputs. Sources: [webuild.io, Calculator UX for fintech](https://webuild.io/calculator-ux-design-for-fintech/); [CALCONIC best practices](https://www.calconic.com/blog/web-calculator-best-practices); [productagency.com.au on calculator UX](https://www.productagency.com.au/insights/great-online-calculator-ux-and-design).
- **Anxiety-reducing UX** (users arrive mid-purchase, worried about doing it wrong): reduce choices per screen, show the system's confidence honestly, give a clear "you are done, here is your number" endpoint. Source: [UXmatters, "Designing Calm: UX Principles for Reducing Users' Anxiety," May 2025](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2025/05/designing-calm-ux-principles-for-reducing-users-anxiety.php).
- **Sliders**: NN/g's standing guidance — sliders suit *approximate* values; precise values on touch are hard (thumb occlusion, motor precision, accidental release), so pair sliders with stepper buttons/detents and show the value large and away from the thumb. Sources: [NN/g, Slider Design: Rules of Thumb](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gui-slider-controls/); [Smashing Magazine, Designing the Perfect Slider, 2017](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2017/07/designing-perfect-slider/).

**Read for OVS:** the 6"-snap width control + wall/island segmented toggle is exactly the right control grammar (discrete detents = approximate-friendly). The homeowner persona needs a *terminus*: a plain-language "your answer" block (already partly served by verdict stamps + CFM band) and a way to carry it away (print/copy/share a spec line). Anxious buyers don't want a sandbox; they want the shortest honest path to a number they can trust — trust being supplied by the citations and the neutral-body framing.

### 1.5 What "success" looks like for a content site without commerce

- Industry guidance converges on: **returning visitors** ("one-time traffic offers limited value; retention signals content quality"), scroll/completion depth, and goal-anchored micro-conversions rather than raw time-on-page. Sources: [agencyanalytics.com, content metrics 2025](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/content-marketing-metrics); [Semrush, Content Performance](https://www.semrush.com/blog/content-performance/); [Optimizely, 20 website metrics](https://www.optimizely.com/insights/blog/website-metrics/).
- For OVS specifically the honest success stack is: **(1) time-to-answer** (persona a: seconds from landing to a defensible CFM number — measurable in-lab, proxied live by scroll-to-answer depth), **(2) instrument engagement rate** (any control touched — proxy for persona b), **(3) return visits + citation pickup** (AI-answer citations per the AI expert report §1.3, inbound links, GSC impressions on question queries). No 2025-2026 source proposes a standard "time-to-answer" benchmark for content sites — treat the 60-second target as an internal product requirement, not an industry number. **[internal target, not an external benchmark]**
- Note: analytics wiring is explicitly out of scope of v2 (spec §11), so every recommendation in §3/§4 below must be justifiable *without* live metrics — lab observation and research citations only.

### 2.0 What is genuinely good (don't regress this)

- **The answer-first pattern works end to end.** The homepage question cards *contain the numbers* ("…needs a minimum of 1,200 CFM, with 1,500 CFM as the recommended…" right on card 2.1), and every question page opens with a boxed ANSWER containing the number above the fold at 375px (`hasNumberAboveFold: true`, `question-cfm-mobile-fold.png`). This is exactly the Tse/Aisch rule (§1.2) implemented correctly: no retrieval behind interaction.
- **Measured time-to-answer for "48-inch island hood CFM," cold mobile landing:** land → scroll 2.4 viewports to §2.0 Questions (y=1,951 of 8,828) → tap card 2.1 → answer box fully above the fold with numbers; island variant one heading below ("Why island installs need more": 1,450 min / 1,800 rec). Three gestures, **~30–45 s reading time; ~15 s on desktop** (cards contain numbers without any click). The 60-second budget is met with margin. (`home-mobile-light-full.png`, `question-cfm-mobile-full.png`)
- **Instruments are real and honest.** Keyboard: focusing the wind slider and stepping it updates the capture readout (86%→79%) with `aria-live=polite` readouts and stamp. Reduced motion: zero particle circles, static plume silhouette rendered (`tool-i01-reduced-motion.png`). Dark theme is first-class, ember-orange escape particles legible (`tool-i01-desktop-dark.png`). 375px: sliders 44px tall, drag strips 46×46, `touch-action: none` so drags don't fight scroll; paper tables scroll inside their own containers with no page-level horizontal scroll.
- **Ask box degrades honestly.** Submitting locally (worker unreachable) yields "AI search is temporarily unavailable. Try browsing the research library or instruments below" + two escape links (`askbox-after-submit.png`). Trust framing under the box ("Answers are generated from research briefs RB-001–RB-012 and cite their clauses. Nothing outside the corpus.") is the right voice.
- **404 is on-voice** ("NOTE — 404. This clause does not exist in the standard.") and the footer covers most of the site (`404-desktop.png`).

### 2.1 Persona (a) — homeowner, phone, mid-planning, "what CFM do I need" in 60 seconds

**Journey: PASS on content, friction on chrome.**

1. **Land (375×812):** headline + mission + NOTE box + ask box all in the first ~1.8 screens; ask box top at y=597 — discoverable on first scroll. Good. But the header nav shows only "2.0 Questions" plus a 19px sliver of "3.0 Instruments": the nav is a horizontally scrollable strip (`scrollWidth` 544 vs visible 117px) — **Research, Standard, About and the Search link (x=481, off a 375px viewport) are invisible**. The only signifiers are that sliver and a 20px fade (`components.css` §.ovs-nav-wrap::after). No hamburger, no wrap. A phone user has no reliable way to know 60% of the site exists from the header. (`mobile-nav-header.png`, measured link positions)
2. **Hero instrument on mobile** occupies y=779→~1,950 — nearly 1.5 screens *between* the ask box and the question cards. It's beautiful, but for this persona it is 1.5 screens of physics between them and their question. The section heading "2.0 Start with your question" is exactly right — it just arrives late.
3. **Question page:** boxed answer above fold, byline immediately below (trust at the decision moment — good), then the i02 embed with preset chips ("Compact wall kitchen / Standard island / Big island, exposed / Pro outdoor kitchen" — story-named, good). **But:** the embed defaults to `wall-48` on a page whose biggest fork is island-vs-wall, and on mobile the instrument stack (chart → chips → 4 controls → readouts) is ~2 viewports tall, so **tapping a preset chip changes readouts that are off-screen** — the feedback loop that makes an instrument an instrument is severed at 375px. The verdict/answer moment happens where the user isn't looking.
4. **The i02 embed and tool page render no verdict stamp.** Code review shows this is a *deliberate, documented* deviation (`i02.mjs` header: a verdict on the computed requirement would be tautological — the instrument outputs the requirement, there's nothing to grade). The physics honesty is correct. But it leaves the homeowner's central anxiety — **"is the hood I'm about to buy enough?"** — unanswered by the one instrument aimed at them. The honest, non-tautological fix exists: let the user enter *their candidate hood's rated CFM* and grade *that* against the computed requirement (pure comparison, no new physics). See W5-T4.
5. **No carry-away.** After configuring 48"/island/60k BTU and getting 1,800 CFM, there is no "copy this spec," no print stylesheet anywhere in the CSS bundle (`grep "@media print"` → 0 across tokens/base/components), no way to hand the number to a spouse/contractor except a screenshot. The site answers the question and then drops the handoff.

### 2.2 Persona (b) — curious tinkerer: will they discover the toys?

**The fun exists but is systematically under-signaled.**

- **Drag affordances are invisible.** The v2.1 drag targets (hood edge, wind arrow) are fully transparent rects (`fill: rgba(0,0,0,0)`, no visible handle geometry, no `aria-label`, no `role`) whose only signifiers are a desktop-only `cursor: ew-resize` on hover and a `:hover` style (`viz.mjs` lines ~600–622; `components.css` §1626+). On touch there is **zero** indication dragging exists. Research (§1.3) is unambiguous: *"unless users have prior knowledge that a gesture exists, they won't try."* The feature Mark paid for in v2.1-B is effectively desktop-hover-Easter-egg-only. Because sliders remain the primary path, nothing is *lost* — but nothing is *found* either.
- **Preset chips read as metadata, not invitations.** Computed style: 10.9px monospace, muted grey `#5c6470` on white, 1px hairline border, 2px radius, 34px tall, no row label (`chipRowLabel: ""`). They share the visual language of the readout/clause chips used *everywhere else on the site for non-interactive labels* — the archetypal weak-signifier problem (§1.3). They also sit *above* the controls looking like filter tags. `aria-pressed` is wired (good); the visual grammar isn't. A one-word label ("Try:") plus button-weight styling changes their read entirely.
- **The verdict stamp is the best affordance on the site** — the rotated PASS slam is noticeable, `aria-live`, threshold explanation present… in a `title` tooltip (*"Plume capture 86% — model-criterion thresholds 85% PASS / 60% MARGINAL"*). Per Tse's rule (§1.2), assume no one ever sees it. On-stamp text "model criterion · capture per RB-005 §2.2" is standards-voice but gives a homeowner no way to parse PASS ≠ "my purchase is validated." One visible plain-language line ("86% of smoke captured in this modeled scene") closes both the comprehension gap and the honesty risk.
- **Homepage does invite exploration structurally** (hero I-01 with live controls above the fold on desktop is a strong "this site is alive" signal — `home-desktop-light-fold.png`), and the instruments grid sells the catalog. The tinkerer who arrives on desktop will have a great time; on mobile they may never learn the figures move beyond sliders.
- **"Explain this configuration"** renders as a detached button floating 70px *below* the instrument frame, left-aligned, outside the `<figure>` (`explainBtn.insideFigure: false`). Scope is ambiguous (explain what? the page? the figure?), and it's the single least-discoverable placement for the site's most novel AI feature. It belongs inside the instrument chrome, next to the readouts it narrates.

### 2.3 Persona (c) — contractor/installer verifying a spec

- **The verification trail is genuinely strong:** byline + published/updated dates at the answer, numbered references resolving to RB papers, "How to Cite" blocks, CSV downloads (RB-008: "Download data (CSV)" present), "Where this research is applied" cross-links. W2's tool-page "Reference readings" tables are **helping, not burying**: on `cfm-calculator` the instrument ends at y≈1,102 and the table sits at y≈1,622 under "Method & assumptions" — exactly where a verifier looks and a homeowner never scrolls. Same anatomy on I-01. Keep this ordering.
- **RB-008 is a 40,370px-tall page with 33 tables and a TOC that does not stick** (`position: static` — design spec §4 explicitly promised "sticky clause-numbered TOC"). A contractor hunting Table 3.2c gets a 50-viewport scroll with no persistent wayfinding and no back-to-top. This is the contractor persona's single biggest friction, and it's a spec regression, not a design decision.
- **Search:** header link exists (desktop), Fuse.js works, AI toggle present. But relevance is off: query "island hood cfm" returns the island-vs-wall question first and **does not surface "What CFM does my outdoor kitchen hood need?" in the top 5** (`search-results.png`) — title/keyword weighting needs a pass. On mobile, the Search link is one of the nav items lost off-screen (2.1.1); footer is the only reliable route.
- **No print stylesheet** (see 2.1.5): printing an answer or method section for a job folder produces raw screen layout.
- **Footer gap:** the footer lists Research briefs / Methodology / References / Downloads / Instruments / Search / Governance / Terms / About — **but not Questions**, the site's primary content type and its answer layer.

### 2.4 Cosmetic/consistency observations (both themes)

- **Exposed grey grid backgrounds read as broken:** the hero instrument's control grid paints its container background (`rgb(216,218,217)`) wherever `auto-fit` tracks leave a gap — at 1440 a ~370×140px flat grey rectangle sits beside SIDE PANELS (`home-desktop-light-fold.png`, bottom right of instrument); the homepage instruments grid (11 cards) leaves a similar grey slab after I-11 (`home-desktop-light-full.png`). Looks like an unloaded component, in both themes.
- **Stale copy:** homepage instruments section still says "Every instrument is **being rebuilt** as a live visualization…" — they shipped. On a site whose brand is precision, tense matters.
- **404** has a home link only — no search box, no question links; a dead end one link deep (`404-desktop.png`).
- Mixed absolute (`https://outdoorventilationstandard.com/...`) nav/footer hrefs vs relative card hrefs — invisible to users in production, but it makes local/preview walkthroughs subtly wrong and is worth normalizing. **[minor]**
- i05 correctly renders two per-side stamps (FAIL/PASS side-by-side) — the comparison payoff is excellent (`tool-i05-desktop.png`).

## 4. Proposed W5 UX wave

**Thesis:** v2.1 built the right instruments; W5 makes them *findable, legible, and conclusive* — nav you can see, feedback you can see, affordances you can see, and an answer you can take with you. Ordered by leverage; each task is independently shippable and testable. All inherit v2/v2.1 global constraints (39/39 physics tests untouched, readouts remain exact module outputs, keyboard parity, reduced-motion parity, Lighthouse ≥95, CLS 0, no brand names, no product recommendations).

- **W5-T1 — Mobile chrome: disclosure nav + search slot** (P0-1, P1-8-reachability). Files: `header.html` partial, `components.css`, small vanilla JS (no framework). Verify: puppeteer 375/768/1440 both themes; keyboard trap check; all 5 sections + search reachable at 375 in ≤2 taps.
- **W5-T2 — Sticky live readout strip on narrow instruments** (P0-2). Engine-level, opt-in spec key (`spec.stickyReadout: [controlIds]`), adopted by i01–i03/i05/i07/i08 first. Verify: chip tap at 375 shows value+grade change without scroll; readouts still equal node-computed values; CLS 0.
- **W5-T3 — Signifier pass: drag handles + hint pulse + chips-as-invitations + on-surface verdict explanation** (P1-1, P1-2, P1-3, P1-7). One coordinated visual-language task so the drafting aesthetic stays coherent. Verify: reduced-motion (no pulse), contrast both themes, 44px targets, stamp line matches physics readout exactly, per-instrument puppeteer sweep.
- **W5-T4 — I-02 "Check a hood" comparison input** (P1-4). Physics-honest comparison of user-entered rated CFM vs computed requirement; verdict semantics documented in `i02.mjs` header (resolves the documented deviation with a non-tautological grade). Needs one Mark decision: grade against *recommended* or *minimum* as the PASS line (suggest: PASS ≥ recommended, MARGINAL ≥ minimum, else FAIL — matches RB-008's own framing). Verify: node-vs-DOM readout equality at 6 configurations, keyboard, SR announcement.
- **W5-T5 — Contractor wayfinding: sticky clause TOC + back-to-top on papers** (P1-5). Verify: RB-008 desktop scroll-spy; mobile floating TOC button; no CLS.
- **W5-T6 — Carry-away: copy-spec-line + print stylesheet** (P1-6). Verify: clipboard content equals live readouts; print preview of question + paper pages.
- **W5-T7 — Sweep: grey grid gaps, stale copy, footer Questions link, 404 links, Fuse weighting, relURL, aria-hidden drag rects, P2-5 anchor CTA** (all P2 + P1-8). One agent, one afternoon, screenshot-diffed.

Suggested split: T1+T7 (chrome/sweep agent), T2+T3 (engine/visual agent), T4 (instrument agent), T5+T6 (document-surface agent) — four parallel lanes, no shared files except `components.css` (T3 owns it; T1 takes a partial-scoped file or lands first).

**Deliberately not proposed:** autoplaying demos, onboarding modals/coach-marks (off-voice for a standards body), gamification, moving the ask box above the hero identity (the NOTE-block pledge + identity *is* the trust that makes the ask box usable), any verdict UI beyond what the physics computes, and any homepage reordering on desktop — the desktop fold (identity + live I-01 + ask box) is the strongest thing the site owns.
