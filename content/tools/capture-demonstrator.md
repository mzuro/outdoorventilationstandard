This instrument demonstrates the core capture problem this standard exists to address: whether a buoyant cooking plume, as it rises and expands, stays inside the hood's capture envelope or escapes it. Every other instrument on this site isolates one variable from this same model — this one lets you move all of them at once.

Two things in the table are deliberate. First, under a **side wind** — blowing across the hood face, parallel to a rear wall — the wall and island columns are identical: the wall is parallel to the flow and shelters nothing, so capture is set entirely by the 12 inches of overhang beyond each end of the cooking surface, and it falls from about 87% in still air to 51% at 5 mph and 20% at 8 mph. Second, under a **rear wind** the wall is doing real work: RB-006 finds a solid rear wall cuts the wind the plume feels by 60–80% within its recirculation zone [RB-006 §3.9.2], so the wall-mounted hood still holds about 88% at 8 mph while the island hood — with only 9.5 inches of front-to-back overhang on each side — has fallen to about 12%. That is why RB-006 grades installations by wind exposure and direction, not by hood shape alone [RB-006 §3.8].

The 87% still-air figure is itself a message about hood size. The papers' recommended hood for this grill at 30 inches is 57 inches wide by 53 inches deep (RB-002 Tables 3.6b and 3.7); the 48-inch preset is 84% of that width, which RB-008 Table 3.10 classes as *marginal* — "80–85% at best" — and advises fixing with width, not airflow [RB-008 §3.10]. The default preset is undersized by design so the effect of wind is visible; drag the width control to 57 inches to see the paper's own design point, which the model reads at about 96% in still air and 72% at 5 mph (RB-006 Table 3.10 prints ">95%" and "70–75%" for that hood [RB-006 §3.10]).

## How the demonstrator works

The demonstrator combines three results from the research program into a single interactive model:

1. **Plume width by height** — the Heskestad half-width b_T = 0.12 (z − z_0) from [RB-001](/research/rb-001-buoyant-plume-behavior/) §2.2 and [RB-002](/research/rb-002-entrainment-lateral-plume-spread/) App. A.3, which sets how far the plume has expanded by the time it reaches the hood. The plume at hood height is modeled as Gaussian with σ = 1.5 b_T, the radius of RB-002's time-averaged 90% flux contour [RB-002 §3.4].
2. **Hood geometry and capture** — the aperture is the hood's overhang beyond the cooking surface along the wind axis, exactly as [RB-006](/research/rb-006-wind-interaction-crossflow/) §3.4 states its escape thresholds: a centerline deflected to one b_T inside the hood edge loses about 25% of the plume; to the edge, about 50% [RB-006 §3.4]. The wall-mount rear-wind case reflects the upwind tail per [RB-005](/research/rb-005-hood-geometry-capture/) §3.4.4.
3. **Wind deflection** — the RB-006 §3.1 closed-form deflection δ = 0.35 U_w z / u_0(z), applied to the wind the plume actually feels after any side panels or rear wall [RB-006 §3.1, §3.9].

Default preset is a 48-inch island installation (`island-48`) — no wall, no side panels, so wind sensitivity is at its maximum. This is illustrative, not a design tool: it does not size exhaust airflow, so it should not be used on its own to specify an installation. Use the [Outdoor Range Hood CFM Calculator](/tools/cfm-calculator/) for that.
