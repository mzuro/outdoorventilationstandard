At a 30-inch mounting height, a 5 mph crosswind deflects the plume centerline about 11.7 inches (RB-006 Table 3.2b prints 12) — most of the 17-inch overhang RB-002 and RB-005 recommend for a medium gas grill at that height [RB-002 Table 3.6b; RB-005 §3.1] — and an 8 mph crosswind deflects it about 18.8 inches (Table 3.2b: 19), past that overhang [RB-006 §3.2]. The same crosswind deflects the plume roughly 1.8× farther at a 48-inch mounting height than at 30 inches (21.1 versus 11.7 inches at 5 mph), because the plume rises farther, and more slowly, before it reaches a higher hood [RB-006 §3.1]. Deflection is linear in wind speed at a fixed height: at 30 inches the 12 mph deflection (28.2 inches) is four times the 3 mph deflection (7.0 inches), and the 3 mph figures at 18, 30 and 48 inches (3.8, 7.0 and 12.7 inches) are the RB-003 benchmark the formula was calibrated against (4, 7 and 12 inches) [RB-006 §3.1].

## Key Findings

**3 mph is manageable.** At light breezes the plume deflects about 7 inches at a 30-inch mounting height — well within the 17-inch recommended overhang.

**5 mph is marginal.** For the paper's recommended 57-inch hood over this grill, RB-006 §3.4's thresholds put the 25%-escape wind at about 4.8 mph and centerline exit at about 7.0 mph (computed from the same `physics/capture.mjs` identities; RB-006 Table 3.4a prints higher figures because it was evaluated with a wider, wind-margin hood — see the paper's revision notes). Gusts beyond 5 mph intermittently push the plume outside the hood boundary, producing the gust-correlated smoke escape of FM-4 [RB-006 §4.2; RB-007 §3.5].

**8 mph and above cause failure.** The roughly 19-inch deflection at 30 inches exceeds the recommended overhang; RB-006 puts unmitigated capture at 45–55% here [RB-006 §4.2]. Side panels or wind baffles become mandatory at this exposure level.

**12+ mph is impractical for open hoods.** Above 12 mph at cooking height the plume is in RB-006's disrupted regime (Fr > 2.7 at every standard height by 15 mph). No conventional canopy hood captures this — physical enclosure is required [RB-006 §3.8, §4.2].
